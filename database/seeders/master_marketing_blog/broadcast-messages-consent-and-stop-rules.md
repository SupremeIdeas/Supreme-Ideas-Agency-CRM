Sending one message to many people is powerful and easy to abuse. Respond is built so the safe way is also the easy way.

## Only people who agreed

A broadcast goes only to contacts marked as willing to hear from you. In the inbox you can record that a customer agreed. Everyone else is never included, whatever filters you choose.

## STOP means stop

If a customer replies **STOP**, **unsubscribe** or a similar plain word, they are removed from broadcasts at once. If they write **START** later, they can come back. The match is exact, so a sentence that merely contains the word stop does not unsubscribe anyone.

## Preview before you send

The **preview** shows how many people would receive the message and a few examples, and an estimated cost if you have set one. Nothing is sent and nobody is touched by a preview.

## Schedule and pace

You can send now or schedule for later. Large sends are paced in batches so a channel is not flooded, and you can cancel a running broadcast; anyone not yet reached is skipped.

## Channel rules differ

Telegram, web chat and email allow free-form messages to people who contacted you. Channels with a 24-hour window, such as WhatsApp, Instagram and Messenger, require pre-approved templates for broadcasts. Respond blocks those until templates exist, rather than letting a send fail or break the platform's rules.

## Good habits

- Say who you are in the first line.
- Keep one idea per message.
- Make the benefit clear.
- Send at a sensible hour for your customers.
- Check results afterwards and prune people who never engage.

## Questions people ask

**Do I need consent for email broadcasts too?**
Yes. Only message people who agreed, and make it easy to stop. The same opt-in and STOP rules apply.

**What if someone writes 'please stop messaging me' in a sentence?**
Treat that as a request and mark them as not accepting broadcasts from the conversation. The automatic removal only fires on an exact STOP, to avoid mistakes.

**Can I send to a tagged group?**
Yes. Choose a tag or stage as a segment, then preview the audience before sending.
