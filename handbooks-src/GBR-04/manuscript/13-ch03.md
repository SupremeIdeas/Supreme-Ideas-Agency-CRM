## The Student visa, step by step {.chapter number="3"}

This chapter covers how to apply for a UK Student visa, what you must prove, what it costs, how long it takes, what you can and cannot do once you are in the UK, and what happens after your course. By the end you should be able to prepare a complete application and avoid the most common reasons for refusal.

### What you need to know

The Student visa works on a points system. You need 70 points: 50 for a valid CAS from a licensed sponsor, 10 for meeting the financial requirement and 10 for meeting the English requirement. You must also meet the other rules, including the genuine-student requirement, and have a TB test certificate if you are from a listed country [VERIFY: whether your country is on the TB test list]{.v}.

You apply online. You can apply up to six months before your course starts, and from outside the UK a decision usually takes about three weeks [VERIFY: current standard processing time]{.v}. UK visas are now digital. You get an eVisa linked to your online account rather than a sticker or card, so you prove your status with a share code [VERIFY: current eVisa process for new arrivals]{.v}.

### The money you must show

You must show two things: your first-year tuition fees (minus anything already paid) and your living costs for up to nine months. The funds must be held for at least 28 days in a row, and the 28 days must end within 31 days before you apply [VERIFY: exact timing rule]{.v}. The table shows the monthly living-cost amounts.

If you have lived in the UK for 12 months or more on a valid visa, you may not need to show these funds [VERIFY: who is exempt]{.v}. If an official sponsor such as a government or the British Council pays both your fees and living costs, that can satisfy the requirement as well. If your university has already received some tuition, you only show the unpaid part.

**Where the money can come from.** The funds must be yours, or your parents' or legal guardians', with proof of the relationship and written consent [VERIFY: current rules on whose accounts are accepted]{.v}. Do not borrow money just to show it, and do not use a loan unless it is an approved education loan that meets the rules. Do not move money in for the 28 days and take it out after. The Home Office can check, and fake or borrowed funds can lead to a refusal and a ban of up to 10 years.

### English language

For most new applicants from 8 January 2026, you need to show English at CEFR level B2, usually with a Secure English Language Test from an approved provider [VERIFY: approved tests, exemptions and whether your degree can be accepted]{.v}. Some students are exempt, for example if they hold a qualifying degree taught in English from a majority English-speaking country. The university may also set its own English requirement, and it may be higher than the Home Office level.

The IHS gives you access to the NHS during your stay. You still pay for things such as prescriptions in England and some dental care. Chapter 18 in Part V covers health and the NHS.

### Step by step: apply for the Student visa

1.  **Receive your CAS.** Check the name, date of birth, passport number, course, dates and tuition on it. Tell the university at once if anything is wrong.

2.  **Check your funds** meet the rule and that the 28-day period is covered by your statements.

3.  **Book and take your English test and, if required, your TB test.** Allow time for results.

4.  **Complete the online application** on GOV.UK, enter your CAS number and answer every question truthfully.

5.  **Pay the visa fee and the IHS** through the official site.

6.  **Book biometrics.** You provide your fingerprints and photo at an approved centre, or use the Home Office app where allowed [VERIFY: how your country applicants give biometrics]{.v}.

7.  **Upload documents** such as passport, CAS details, financial evidence, English evidence, TB certificate and any consent letters.

8.  **Wait for a decision,** and keep your phone and email active. The Home Office may ask for extra documents or an interview.

9.  **After approval,** check your eVisa details, including dates and conditions, as soon as you can access it.

### Two documents that can delay you: ATAS and the TB test

**ATAS.** Some postgraduate courses and research in sensitive subjects (certain engineering, physics, chemistry, computing and advanced technology fields) need an Academic Technology Approval Scheme certificate for students from most countries. It is free, but it takes at least 30 working days and longer in the summer, and you must have it **before** you apply for the Student visa. Ask the university's admissions team, in writing, whether your exact course needs one. If it does, apply the day you receive your offer, because the CAS cannot be used without it [VERIFY: GOV.UK ATAS guidance; the university may issue the CAS only once the certificate is in hand]{.v}.

**TB test.** If you are applying from a country on the Home Office list and your stay will be longer than six months, you must submit a certificate from an approved clinic. The list includes Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya, South Africa, Uganda, Tanzania, Zambia, Zimbabwe, Cameroon, Ethiopia, Rwanda, Senegal and many other African countries. Only clinics approved for the Home Office can issue the certificate, and each country has its own clinic list on GOV.UK [VERIFY: your country on the list; certificate validity; clinic fee]{.v}. The certificate is valid for a limited time, so book it after your English test and before your funds period ends, not months earlier.

### What the application asks, and how to answer

Most Student visa questions are factual: your passport, your CAS number, your money, your English evidence and your travel history. Three tips make the difference between a clean application and a refusal.

- **Answer from your documents.** Have your passport, CAS, bank statements and test certificate open and copy the details exactly. A date of birth or passport number that differs by one digit creates a mismatch.
- **Declare everything you are asked about.** Previous visa refusals anywhere in the world, previous UK visits, criminal history and any overstay must be declared honestly. Leaving something out is treated as deception and can mean a 10-year ban, while declaring a refusal with a short, truthful explanation is normal.
- **Prove who you are.** You will prove your identity as part of the application, either by an app-based check or at a visa application centre, depending on your nationality and passport [VERIFY: current identity process for your country]{.v}.

::: {.plain title="Faster decisions"}
A standard decision from outside the UK usually takes about three weeks. A paid priority service may be offered when you apply, and you are told if you are eligible. It speeds up the decision but does not improve your chances, and you should not pay anyone other than the Home Office for it [VERIFY: priority fee and eligibility]{.v}.
:::

### What officers look at in the genuine student check

Home Office officers and your university both ask whether you are a genuine student who will study and then leave or switch visas lawfully. They look at patterns. A strong application tells a consistent story.

- **A clear link between your past and your course.** A first degree in accountancy and a master's in finance is easy to explain. A move from nursing to cybersecurity needs a thoughtful reason.
- **Why this university and why the UK.** Specific reasons (a module, a lecturer, a placement scheme, a cost comparison) read better than "it is a good university".
- **Your finances and sponsors.** The money has a clear source, and any sponsor can be identified and has consented.
- **Your plans after the course.** Show a realistic plan at home or an honest path to another visa. Do not promise to return if you intend to stay, or the reverse.
- **Your compliance history.** Past visa refusals, overstays and breaches are weighed heavily.

::: {.example title="Telling a consistent story"}
A candidate with a degree in microbiology applies for an MSc in public health. Her statement explains that she worked in a state laboratory for two years, saw gaps in outbreak reporting, and chose a one-year course with a module on infectious-disease surveillance that links to her job. Her employer's letter agrees with her CV, and her funds come from her savings and her father's account, with his consent letter and proof of the relationship. Every document tells the same story.
:::

### Genuine student and credibility checks

The Home Office and your university both look at whether you are a genuine student. They may ask why you chose this course and this university, how it fits your education and career, and how you will pay. Answer in your own words, and make sure your answers match your documents. Inconsistent answers cause refusals even when the funds are fine.

### After you are approved: your eVisa, travel and conditions

UK Student visas are now digital. You get an eVisa linked to your UKVI account, and you prove your right to study and work with a share code, not with a card or sticker [VERIFY: current process for newly approved applicants]{.v}. As soon as you can, sign in and check your name, dates and conditions, and link the passport you will travel on. If any detail is wrong, tell the university and the Home Office before you fly.

Keep the decision email, your CAS and your funds evidence in your hand luggage. The airline may ask for them, and the border officer may ask where you will live, how you will pay and why you chose the course.

**Your conditions are rules, not suggestions.** The Home Office expects you to do the following, and your university reports to it if you do not:

- **Enrol and attend.** Register on time, attend teaching and keep up with your course. A sponsor must report students who fail to enrol or who stop attending.
- **Keep your details up to date.** Tell the university your UK address and phone number within the time it sets, and tell it when they change.
- **Stay within the work limits.** Do not exceed your weekly hours in term time. Never work in a job your visa bans.
- **Do not claim public funds.** A Student visa carries a "no recourse to public funds" condition, so you cannot claim most state benefits or housing assistance.
- **Do not change course or university without checking.** A switch of course or sponsor needs a new CAS and a visa application in most cases.
- **Leave, extend or switch before the visa ends.** The date on your eVisa is the deadline, and there is no grace period.

::: {.warn}
Breaking a condition, even by accident, can lead the Home Office to shorten or cancel your visa. If you are unsure, ask your university's international advice team first. Their advice is free and confidential.
:::

### Working and dependants

-   **Work during your course.** Students on a degree-level course can usually work up to 20 hours a week in term time and full-time in official vacations. Check your visa conditions, because some courses allow less or none [VERIFY: your visa's work conditions]{.v}.

-   **Not allowed:** being self-employed, working as a professional sportsperson or coach, and some other jobs.

-   **Dependants.** Most undergraduate and taught postgraduate students cannot bring a partner or children. PhD and some government-funded students can [VERIFY]{.v}. Each dependant needs their own application, fees and funds.

### After your course: the Graduate Route

The Graduate Route lets you stay and work, or look for work, after you finish a UK degree. For bachelor's and master's graduates, it is two years if you apply on or before 31 December 2026 and 18 months if you apply on or after 1 January 2027. PhD graduates get three years. It cannot be extended, so you must switch to another visa, such as a Skilled Worker visa, if you want to stay longer. The Graduate visa application fee is £937, and the Immigration Health Surcharge for Graduate visa holders is the full rate of £1,035 for each year, not the £776 student rate. GOV.UK lists the surcharge for an 18-month visa as £1,152.50, although one year at £1,035 plus six months at half rate would be £1,552.50, so use the official calculator before you budget [VERIFY: 18-month surcharge on the GOV.UK Graduate visa page]{.v}. Chevening scholars are not eligible.

You must apply while you still hold your Student visa and after your university has told the Home Office you completed your course. Do not let your Student visa expire before you apply.

### Common reasons for refusal

-   Funds not held for the full 28 days, or not in the right form.

-   Money that does not appear to belong to you, or documents that cannot be verified.

-   Missing, wrong or late English or TB evidence.

-   Answers that do not match your documents, or an unclear reason for choosing the course.

-   Using an agent who submitted false or inconsistent information.

If you are refused, you get a refusal letter that states the reasons. You may have a right to an administrative review in some cases, but there are strict time limits [VERIFY: review options and deadlines]{.v}. Often, reapplying with corrected documents is faster. Do not reapply before you understand the reason. Ask your university's international office or a regulated adviser.

::: {.example title="Funds timing"}
A student has £29,000 in her account on 10 January. She applies on 25 January. Her statements show £29,000 on 10 January, but £22,000 on 3 January, before a transfer arrived. The 28-day period is not covered, so the application is likely to be refused. If she had waited until 7 February, the 28 days would have been covered, and she would have lost nothing.
:::

::: {.checklist title="My student visa application"}
-   CAS received and every detail checked.
-   Funds in the right account, covering the full 28 days, using the correct monthly rate for my application date.
-   English evidence valid and accepted.
-   TB test certificate if required.
-   Passport valid for the whole course and not close to expiry.
-   Consent letters and proof of relationship if parents or sponsors supply funds.
-   Answers consistent with my documents and my reason for the course.
:::

### Mistakes that cost people money or time

-   Applying before the 28-day funds period is fully covered.

-   Using the old monthly funds amount after 30 November 2026.

-   Waiting until the last month to book English or TB tests.

-   Letting someone else fill in the application using their own email or phone number.

-   Travelling before the application is decided without checking what happens to the application.
