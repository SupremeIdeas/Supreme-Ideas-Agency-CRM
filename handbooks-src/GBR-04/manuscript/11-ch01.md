## What studying in the UK involves in 2026 {.chapter number="1"}

This chapter explains how the UK system is set up, what it costs in total, and what changed recently. By the end you should be able to tell whether the UK is realistic for you, and roughly how much money you need to have ready.

### What you need to know

**The basic structure.** An undergraduate degree (a bachelor's) usually takes three years in England, Wales and Northern Ireland and four years in Scotland. A taught master's usually takes one year. A research degree such as a PhD usually takes three to four years. Many courses start in September or October. Some postgraduate courses also start in January, and a few start at other times [VERIFY: intake dates for your university]{.v}.

**Home fees and international fees.** Universities charge two different rates. Students who count as "home" students pay the lower rate. Almost everyone coming from Africa to the UK for study pays the international rate, which is set by each university and is much higher. The university tells you which rate applies to you in your offer.

**You can only study on a Student visa at a licensed sponsor.** A university or college must hold a sponsor licence from the Home Office before it can issue the document you need for your visa. If a college is not on the official register, you cannot get a Student visa for it. Chapter 2 shows how to check.

::: {.plain title="Three terms you will see everywhere"}
**UCAS** is the central application system for undergraduate courses. **CAS** (Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies) is the electronic document your university gives you after you accept an offer and meet its conditions. You cannot apply for your Student visa without it. **Sponsor** is the university or college that issues your CAS and takes responsibility for reporting on you to the Home Office.
:::

### What changed recently

Several rules changed in the last year. If you read an older guide, some of what it says will be out of date. The table lists the ones that matter most.

| **Date**         | **Change**                                                                                                                                                                  |
|--------------------|----------------------------------------------------|
| 11 November 2025 | Living-cost funds required for a Student visa rose to £1,529 a month in London and £1,171 a month outside London, for up to nine months.                                    |
| 8 January 2026   | The English language requirement for new Student visa applicants rose from CEFR level B1 to B2 in most cases.                                                               |
| 8 April 2026     | The Student visa application fee rose from £524 to £558.                                                                                                                    |
| 30 November 2026 | Living-cost funds will rise again to £1,570 a month in London and £1,203 a month outside London. This applies to applications made on or after that date.                   |
| 1 January 2027   | The Graduate Route will be shortened from two years to 18 months for bachelor's and master's graduates who apply on or after this date. PhD graduates keep three years.   |
| 1 August 2028    | A new international student levy of £925 per student per year starts for universities in England. Universities pay it, not students, but it may affect fees and admissions. |

::: src
Sources: GOV.UK and Home Office announcements, UKCISA, university international offices and education news, October 2026. Check GOV.UK before you rely on any figure.
:::

**Dependants.** Most undergraduates and taught postgraduates cannot bring a spouse or children on a Student visa. The main exceptions are students on research degrees such as a PhD and students on certain government-funded scholarships [VERIFY: current dependant rules]{.v}. If you are planning to move with family, check this first.

**Compliance.** The Home Office has tightened the rules for universities, so many universities are stricter about who they accept and how they check your documents [VERIFY: current sponsor compliance thresholds]{.v}. Be ready for more questions from the university about your education history and reasons for choosing the course.

### What it costs in total

Tuition is the biggest cost, and it varies a lot by course. As a rough guide, international undergraduate fees at many universities fall between about £18,000 and £30,000 a year, and taught master's fees between about £17,000 and £35,000, with medicine, dentistry and some business and technology courses higher [VERIFY: check each course page; these are editorial estimates]{.v}. Always use the exact figure on the university's own course page.

Then add living costs, the visa fee and the health surcharge. The visa funds rule is a minimum for the visa. It is not a full budget. A student in a city outside London will usually spend more than the minimum once travel, books, phone and social costs are added.

| **Cost**                                   | **Amount**                              | **Approx. US\$** | **When it applies**                          |
|---------------------------|-----------------|--------------|---------------|
| Student visa application fee               | £558                                    | US\$737          | Once, per application                        |
| Immigration Health Surcharge               | £776 per year                           | US\$1,024        | Paid with the application for the whole visa |
| Living-cost funds to show (outside London) | £1,171 a month, up to 9 months, £10,539 | US\$13,911       | Before 30 Nov 2026                           |
| Living-cost funds to show (outside London) | £1,203 a month, up to 9 months, £10,827 | US\$14,292       | From 30 Nov 2026                             |
| Living-cost funds to show (London)         | £1,570 a month, up to 9 months, £14,130 | US\$18,652       | From 30 Nov 2026                             |

::: src
Sources: GOV.UK, UKCISA and university international offices, October 2026. The IHS is charged for each year of visa and a part-year; Chapter 3 explains. £1 = US\$1.32.
:::

Those figures leave out flights, the first month of rent and deposit, a TB test where required, English test fees, document translation, and the cost of the first weeks before you earn anything. Chapter 8 in Part II builds a full pre-departure budget. If your funds are close to the minimum, you will be tight for the whole year.

### How UK degrees are built

Before you compare courses, learn the vocabulary. It stops you paying for the wrong thing and lets you read a university website without guessing.

| Qualification | Typical length | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Foundation year or international foundation programme | 1 year | A bridging year for students whose school qualification does not meet direct entry. Often leads to a named degree at the same university. |
| Bachelor's degree (BA, BSc, LLB, BEng) | 3 years; 4 in Scotland, or 4 with a placement year | The standard undergraduate degree. Most carry "honours" and are graded. |
| Integrated master's (MEng, MSci, MChem) | 4 years | A bachelor's and a master's in one course, common in engineering and sciences. |
| Taught master's (MSc, MA, MBA, LLM) | 1 year full time; some 2 years | Lectures, seminars, exams or coursework and a dissertation. The most popular route for international students. |
| Research master's (MRes, MPhil) | 1 to 2 years | Mostly independent research with some training. Often a stepping stone to a PhD. |
| Doctorate (PhD) | 3 to 4 years | An original research thesis, supervised by an academic. |

::: {.src}
Compiled from UCAS, university course pages and the Quality Assurance Agency framework, October 2026. Course lengths vary by university.
:::

**Credits and levels.** A full-time undergraduate year is usually 120 credits, and a taught master's is usually 180 credits, of which about 60 are the dissertation. Years of an undergraduate degree are called levels 4, 5 and 6, and a master's is level 7. You will see "level 7" on course pages and, later, on job applications and visa forms.

**How degrees are graded.** An honours degree is awarded in classes, and employers and postgraduate admissions tutors read them the same way everywhere in the UK.

| Result | Usual percentage | Often called |
|---|---|---|
| First-class honours | 70% and above | "a First" |
| Upper second | 60% to 69% | "a 2:1" |
| Lower second | 50% to 59% | "a 2:2" |
| Third-class honours | 40% to 49% | "a Third" |
| Master's with Distinction, Merit or Pass | 70%, 60% and 50% and above | Pass mark is usually 50% at master's level |

::: {.src}
Typical UK conventions; each university publishes its own regulations. Check your university's assessment regulations.
:::

A 2:1 or above is the usual entry standard for a taught master's at a good university, and a First or high 2:1 for a research degree. A degree at the level of a 2:2 still opens many doors, but fewer funding options.

::: {.insight}
When a university website says "2:1 or equivalent", it means the equivalent in your country's grading. For a Nigerian, Ghanaian or Kenyan degree, a Second Class Upper or a strong CGPA is usually read as a 2:1, but each university publishes its own country table. Search the university's name plus "international entry requirements" and your country, and compare the result with the UK ENIC comparison service [VERIFY: UK ENIC statement of comparability fee and turnaround]{.v}.
:::

### Your qualification and the UK entry ladder

Most African school-leaving certificates (WAEC and NECO in Nigeria, WASSCE in Ghana, KCSE in Kenya, the National Senior Certificate in South Africa) are not the same as UK A-levels. UK universities therefore usually offer one of three routes. Read the entry page for your country on each university's website and write down which route applies to you.

1. **Direct entry** to year one of a bachelor's degree. Some universities accept a strong national certificate, a good score in specified subjects, or one year of university study already completed.
2. **Foundation year or international foundation programme.** This is the most common route for school leavers. It costs a year of fees and living costs, but it also gives you time to adapt to UK study and often guarantees progression to the degree.
3. **A-levels or the International Baccalaureate first.** If your school offers Cambridge International A-levels or the IB, you are on the same ladder as UK applicants.

For a master's, you generally need a bachelor's degree with the right class and a related subject. If your degree is in a different subject, say so and explain why in your personal statement. A short bridging module or a pre-master's course may be offered.

### Where in the UK: four nations, many cities

Choosing a city changes your budget more than choosing a course does. A table can only be a guide, because the right city depends on your subject, your budget and what you want daily life to feel like.

| City or area | Feel | What to expect on cost |
|---|---|---|
| London | Huge, international, many universities and jobs, expensive | The highest rent and transport costs. The Home Office funds figure is higher here. |
| Manchester, Birmingham, Leeds, Sheffield | Large English cities with several universities and strong student life | Rents well below London, lively part-time job markets. |
| Glasgow, Edinburgh | Scottish cities; Scotland has a four-year bachelor's | Edinburgh is pricier. Scottish rules differ for housing and for UK residents' fees. |
| Cardiff, Swansea | Welsh cities; Wales has its own housing rules | Often cheaper than England for the same quality of life. |
| Belfast | Northern Ireland; lower living costs | Rents are among the lowest, and there is a growing technology and finance sector. |
| Smaller university towns (Exeter, Lancaster, Aberdeen, Coventry and others) | Compact, campus-centred, close to nature or heritage | Cheaper rent but fewer part-time jobs; check transport links. |

### Is the UK realistic for you?

Answer these four questions honestly before you pay application fees.

1.  **Can you show the money?** You need the full first-year tuition plus living costs available in your own account for 28 days. If you cannot, look at scholarships (Chapter 2) or a cheaper course.

2.  **Do you meet the English requirement?** Most applicants need B2 evidence (Chapter 3).

3.  **Do you have a clear reason for this course?** Universities and the Home Office both check that you are a genuine student. A course that does not match your history needs a clear explanation.

4.  **Do you have a plan after the course?** The Graduate Route is shorter from 2027 and cannot be extended (Chapter 3). Do not plan your whole finances around staying to work.

::: {.example title="Checking the numbers early"}
A student has an offer for a one-year master's outside London. The tuition is £18,000. She calculates the funds she must show as £18,000 plus £10,827, a total of £28,827. She has £24,000 saved. She sees the gap early and applies to two other courses with fees of £14,500 and £15,200, and to a university scholarship. Doing this before paying a deposit saves her from a failed visa application.
:::

::: {.checklist title="Is my plan realistic?"}
-   I know the exact tuition fee for my course from the university's own page.
-   I have calculated the funds I must show using the right monthly amount for my application date.
-   I know my English evidence and when it expires.
-   I know whether my course allows dependants if I plan to bring any.
-   I have a back-up course or university in case an offer or visa does not work out.
:::

### Mistakes that cost people money or time

-   Using last year's visa funds figure. The amount changes, and from 30 November 2026 it is higher.

-   Paying a large tuition deposit before checking the refund terms if the visa is refused.

-   Choosing a course because it was the quickest to admit, not because it fits their background. This can cause problems at the genuine-student stage.

-   Assuming a spouse or children can come. Most taught-course students cannot bring dependants.

-   Planning to stay and work after the course as if it were guaranteed. The Graduate Route is shorter from January 2027.
