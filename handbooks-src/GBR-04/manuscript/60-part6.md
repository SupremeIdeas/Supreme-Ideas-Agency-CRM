# After your course, and your toolkit {.part number="VI" sub="The Graduate Route and other visas, going home or moving on, and a toolkit of calendars, checklists, templates, a directory and a glossary."}

## The Graduate Route and other ways to stay {.chapter number="20"}

This chapter explains what your visa options are after you finish your degree: the Graduate Route, the Skilled Worker visa, the High Potential Individual route and others, and how to avoid the mistakes that lose people their right to stay. By the end you should know the dates that matter, the money you will need and how to plan your last year as if it were a job search.

### What you need to know

Your Student visa is for study. When you finish, you must leave, extend, or switch to another route before your permission ends. Many students want to stay and work. Some routes allow it, but none is automatic, and the rules are changing. Plan from the first term, not the last.

| Route | What it is | Length | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Graduate visa | Lets you work or look for work after a UK degree | 18 months for bachelor's and master's graduates who apply on or after 1 January 2027 (2 years if you apply on or before 31 December 2026); 3 years for doctoral graduates | Cannot be extended; no sponsor needed. Not for Chevening scholars. |
| Skilled Worker visa | A job with a licensed sponsor at the right skill level and salary | Up to 5 years, extendable | The general salary threshold is £41,700 or the going rate for the job, whichever is higher, with lower thresholds for some new entrants and some jobs [VERIFY: your job's going rate and the current thresholds]{.v}. |
| High Potential Individual (HPI) visa | For graduates of certain top global universities | 2 years (3 for doctoral graduates) | You must have been awarded a qualification by an eligible university in the last five years [VERIFY: eligible university list]{.v}. |
| Global Talent visa | For leaders or emerging leaders in academia, research, arts and technology | Up to 5 years | Needs an endorsement. |
| Start-up, Innovator Founder and Scale-up routes | For founders and fast-growing businesses | Varies | Need an endorsement or sponsor [VERIFY: current route names and rules]{.v}. |
| Further study | A new Student visa for a higher course | Course length plus | Needs a new CAS and funds. |

::: {.src}
GOV.UK, October 2026. The Graduate visa changes on 1 January 2027. Check each route for the latest rules.
:::

### The Graduate Route in detail

You can apply from within the UK, **after** your university has told the Home Office that you completed your course and **before** your Student visa expires. You must be in the UK when you apply, and you must have studied a qualifying course at a university with a track record of compliance [VERIFY: eligibility rules]{.v}. You can work in almost any job, including self-employment, and you can look for work. You cannot claim most state benefits, and you cannot extend the visa.

| Cost | Amount | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Graduate visa application fee | £937 | Per applicant [VERIFY: GOV.UK]{.v}. |
| Immigration Health Surcharge | £1,035 per year of visa | Full rate, not the student rate; GOV.UK lists £1,152.50 for 18 months, so use the official calculator [VERIFY]{.v}. |
| Total for a two-year visa (applied for before 1 Jan 2027) | £3,007 | £937 plus £2,070. |

::: {.src}
GOV.UK Graduate visa pages, October 2026.
:::

::: {.warn}
Do not let your Student visa expire before you apply. Apply once your university has confirmed completion to the Home Office, which can take a few weeks after your results. Ask your international advice team when the confirmation is sent, so you can apply as early as possible.
:::

### Moving to a Skilled Worker visa

The Skilled Worker route is the main long-term option. You need a job offer from a Home Office licensed sponsor, a Certificate of Sponsorship, a salary at or above the threshold and the going rate for your occupation, and a job at the required skill level. Employers that are licensed sponsors are listed on a government register. New entrants and some graduates qualify for lower salary thresholds for a limited time [VERIFY: current new-entrant rules]{.v}.

1. **Search for sponsors** in your field, using the register of licensed sponsors.
2. **Check the going rate** for the job's occupation code before you apply.
3. **Apply for jobs early,** and tell employers on the first page of your CV that you need sponsorship from a given date.
4. **Start while on the Graduate visa or the Student visa,** whichever is valid, and switch as soon as you have a Certificate of Sponsorship.
5. **Keep your documents** (degree certificate, English evidence, passport) ready.

![Canary Wharf, London's financial district, seen from Greenwich Park. Sectors such as technology, healthcare, engineering and finance have many sponsor employers.](images/GBR-04-c20-city-offices.webp)

### Plan your last year like a project

| When | Action |
|---|---|
| 12 months before the end | Visit the careers service, update your CV, list target sponsors and attend employer events. |
| 9 months before | Apply for graduate schemes and placements, and ask your tutors for references. |
| 6 months before | Check your visa end date, and ask when the university will report completion. |
| 3 months before | Prepare the funds and documents for the Graduate visa, and check the fee and surcharge. |
| Final term | Apply for the Graduate visa as soon as the university confirms your completion. |
| After the Graduate visa begins | Keep applying for sponsored jobs; switch to a Skilled Worker visa when you have an offer. |

### Long-term plans and settlement

Time on a Student or Graduate visa generally does not count towards settlement on a work route, and the government has announced longer qualifying periods for settlement, with the rules still being finalised [VERIFY: current settlement rules]{.v}. If long-term settlement in the UK is your goal, take advice from a regulated adviser, and plan for the possibility that it takes longer than you expected.

::: {.example title="A plan with a deadline"}
A student finishing a one-year master's in September 2027 knows the Graduate visa will be 18 months. In January she starts applying for graduate schemes with sponsors, and she lists four employers on a spreadsheet with their deadlines. She asks the university in July when the completion report will be sent, and applies for her Graduate visa in the week it is confirmed. During the 18 months she plans to secure a Skilled Worker job, and if she does not, she plans to return home with a UK degree and work experience.
:::

::: {.checklist title="Graduate Route readiness"}
- I know my Student visa end date.
- I have asked when my university will report completion.
- I have the money for the visa fee and surcharge.
- I have a list of sponsor employers in my field.
- I know my Plan B if I do not find a sponsor.
- My CV and LinkedIn profile are up to date.
:::

### Mistakes that cost people money or time

- Letting the Student visa expire before applying for the Graduate visa.
- Assuming the Graduate visa can be extended.
- Starting job applications only after the course ends.
- Applying for jobs below the skill or salary level a sponsor can use.
- Taking advice from an unregulated agent.

::: {.verify}
Check before you act: the GOV.UK Graduate visa, Skilled Worker and High Potential Individual pages, the register of licensed sponsors and your university's guidance. The Graduate visa length changes on 1 January 2027. Last verified: 19 October 2026.
:::

::: {.next}
- Write your visa end date and your Graduate visa application window on your calendar.
- List five sponsor employers and their deadlines.
- Book a careers service appointment about sponsored jobs.
:::

## Going home or moving on {.chapter number="21"}

This chapter is for students who will return home, or move to another country, after their course. It covers how to make your UK degree count, how to handle certificates and recognition, how to keep your network alive and how to leave the UK properly. By the end you should have a leaving checklist and a plan for the first three months after you return.

### What you need to know

A UK degree is valuable at home, and so are the skills, contacts and confidence you build. But value does not appear on its own. You need to turn it into evidence: a transcript, a certificate, references, a portfolio and stories about what you did. Going home well is a project too.

### Make your degree count

1. **Collect your documents** before you leave: degree certificate, transcript, diploma supplement, reference letters and your CV.
2. **Ask for certified or verified copies** of your certificate and transcript. Many employers and regulators in your country want them.
3. **Check recognition at home.** If your profession is regulated (medicine, law, engineering, accountancy, teaching), find out what your national body needs for a UK qualification.
4. **Keep a portfolio** of projects, research, presentations and results.
5. **Ask lecturers and supervisors** for references and LinkedIn recommendations before you leave.
6. **Join the alumni network** of your university and, if there is one, your country's alumni group.

![A graduate in a cap. A certified certificate and transcript are what you carry home.](images/GBR-04-c21-graduation.webp)

### Leaving the UK properly

| Task | Why |
|---|---|
| Tell your landlord and accommodation provider | Give the notice your contract requires, return the keys and request your deposit. |
| Close or switch your bank account | Avoid fees, and keep a way to receive refunds or UK payments. |
| Cancel your SIM, subscriptions and bills | Avoid charges after you leave. |
| Settle your council tax and TV licence accounts | Claim any refund. |
| Check your tax | If you worked, you may be due a refund for the part of the year you were here. |
| Update your address with the university | For certificates, transcripts and alumni contact. |
| Keep copies of your UK records | Payslips, tenancy papers and your visa record may help later. |
| Leave on time | Leave before your visa ends, or switch to another visa; overstaying can lead to a re-entry ban. |

### Moving to another country

If you plan to study or work in a third country, check its visa rules early, because a UK degree helps, but it does not replace the visa process. Keep all your academic records and work evidence organised so that you can apply quickly.

### Bringing your UK experience home

Employers at home often value the following, and you should say so in your CV and interviews: independent research, working in international teams, presenting to critical audiences, writing in English to a UK standard, managing your time and money abroad, and understanding how UK and international companies work. Be specific about what you did and the results.

::: {.insight}
Plan your return like a launch. Update your CV, tell your network in your home country that you are coming back, book informational conversations before you land and keep an international number and data plan on arrival, such as a Naara data eSIM, so you can respond to messages and interviews on the first day. A small, well-timed message to a former employer or a professor can open doors.
:::

### If you came on a scholarship with a return condition

Some scholarships, such as Chevening, require you to return to your home country for a period after the course (Chevening requires two years), and they do not allow the Graduate Route [VERIFY: your scholarship's terms]{.v}. Follow the terms. Breaking them can lead to repayment and affect your future applications.

::: {.example title="A return that started months earlier"}
A student finishing in September begins contacting employers in her home city in April. By June she has two informal interviews by video, and a former supervisor introduces her to a research institute. She lands in October with a job offer, a transcript and her references, and she has paid her last UK bills and recovered her deposit.
:::

### Mistakes that cost people money or time

- Leaving the UK without your certificate and transcript.
- Forgetting to claim back deposits and tax.
- Waiting until you are home to start job hunting.
- Assuming your degree is recognised by your professional body without checking.
- Overstaying your visa, even by a few days.

::: {.verify}
Check before you act: your university's graduation and certificates page, your national professional body's recognition rules, HMRC's tax refund guidance and your accommodation contract's moving-out terms. Last verified: 19 October 2026.
:::

::: {.next}
- Start a folder with your certificates, transcripts and references.
- Contact one person in your home country about work this month.
- Check your professional body's rules for UK qualifications.
:::

## The toolkit {.chapter number="22"}

This chapter collects the tools you will use again and again: a master calendar, a one-page checklist, templates you can adapt, a directory of official sources and a glossary. Print it, or save it on your phone.

### Master calendar: from decision to graduation

| Months before the course | What to do |
|---|---|
| 12 to 18 | Choose subjects, research courses and universities, check entry requirements, plan your budget, and look at scholarships. |
| 9 to 12 | Apply to courses (undergraduate through UCAS, postgraduate through university portals) and for scholarships. Prepare your English test. |
| 6 to 9 | Receive offers, check the refund terms, accept an offer and pay any deposit. Apply for accommodation. |
| 4 to 6 | Take your English test, apply for ATAS if needed and plan your funds so the 28 days are covered. |
| 3 to 4 | Receive your CAS, check every detail and book your TB test. |
| 2 to 3 | Apply for your Student visa (up to six months before the course starts), complete biometrics and upload documents. |
| 1 to 2 | Decision, eVisa check, flights, packing, connectivity plan and money for the first month. |
| 0 | Travel, collect keys, enrol, register with a GP, open a bank account. |
| During the course | Attend, study, work within your limits, ask for help, build your career. |
| Last 12 months | Plan the Graduate Route, apply for sponsored jobs and prepare to leave or switch. |

### One-page master checklist

::: {.checklist title="Before I apply for the visa"}
- The university is a licensed sponsor and the offer appears in my own portal.
- My CAS details match my passport.
- My funds cover the amount for my application date for a full 28 days, in an allowed account, with a buffer.
- My English evidence and, if needed, TB and ATAS certificates are valid.
- My documents are translated, scanned and named.
- My passport is valid for the whole course.
- My answers match my documents and my reason for the course.
:::

::: {.checklist title="Before I fly"}
- My eVisa details are correct.
- My accommodation is confirmed in writing.
- My flights match my visa and enrolment dates.
- My phone is unlocked, my data plan is ready and my verification codes work.
- I have documents, medicines and money for the first week.
- My family has my address and emergency contacts.
:::

::: {.checklist title="In my first month"}
- I have enrolled and have my student card.
- I have registered with a GP.
- I have opened a bank account.
- I know my work-hours limit.
- I have joined one society.
- I know how to reach the international advice team, the wellbeing service and campus security.
:::

### Templates

**1. Email to the admissions team about your visa timeline**

> Subject: Questions before I accept my offer (Student ID or application number)
>
> Dear Admissions Team,
> I have an offer for (course) starting in (month, year). Before I accept, please confirm in writing: (1) whether this course needs an ATAS certificate; (2) the deposit amount and what is refunded if my visa is refused; (3) when you expect to issue my CAS once I meet the conditions; (4) the English requirement and whether you accept my test; and (5) whether accommodation is guaranteed.
> Thank you,
> (Full name, date of birth, application number)

**2. Parent or guardian funds consent letter** (adapt to your university's template)

> I, (parent's full name), confirm that I am the (mother, father or legal guardian) of (student's full name), born on (date of birth). I confirm that the funds of (amount in local currency and sterling equivalent) held in my account number (account number) at (bank name) are available to my child to use for tuition fees and living costs while studying (course) at (university) in the United Kingdom. I give my full consent for these funds to be used for this purpose.
> Signed (signature), date (date), contact details (phone and email).

**3. Email to the accommodation team after check-in**

> Subject: Room condition on arrival, (room number)
> Dear Accommodation Team, I checked in on (date). Please find attached photos of the room and the following items that were already damaged or missing: (list). Please add this to my inventory so that I am not charged for it.

### Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ATAS | Academic Technology Approval Scheme, a security certificate for some subjects. |
| CAS | Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies, the electronic document your university issues. |
| CEFR | Common European Framework of Reference for languages, which sets English levels such as B2. |
| eVisa | Your digital immigration record, linked to your UKVI account. |
| Graduate visa | A post-study visa for work or job search. |
| HMO | House in multiple occupation, a shared house with several unrelated tenants. |
| IHS | Immigration Health Surcharge, paid with your visa to use the NHS. |
| OISC | Office of the Immigration Services Commissioner, which regulates immigration advisers. |
| PBSA | Purpose-built student accommodation. |
| SELT | Secure English Language Test, an approved English test for visas. |
| Share code | A code you generate to prove your status to a university, landlord or employer. |
| Sponsor | The university or employer licensed by the Home Office to sponsor your visa. |
| UCAS | The central application system for UK undergraduate courses. |
| UKVI | UK Visas and Immigration, the Home Office division that decides visa applications. |
