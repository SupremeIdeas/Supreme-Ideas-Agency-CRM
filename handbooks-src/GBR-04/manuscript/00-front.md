## How to use this book {.front}

You do not have to read this book from cover to cover. Pick the path that fits where you are now.

### By situation

- **I have not chosen a course yet:** read Chapter 1 for the real cost and rules, then Chapter 2 on choosing and applying.
- **I have an offer and need my visa:** go straight to Chapter 3, then check the 48-hour checklist.
- **I am about to travel:** read Part II (Chapters 4 to 8), then Chapter 10 on your first days.
- **I am already studying in the UK:** go to Part IV on university life, Part V on money, work, health and safety, and Chapter 20 on the Graduate Route.
- **Someone is asking me for money to guarantee admission or a visa:** read Chapter 2's section on fake offers and agents, and Chapter 19, before you pay anything.

### Book map

**Part I** covers how UK study works, how to choose and apply, and the Student visa. **Part II** covers preparing to leave: documents and funds, tests and medicals, travel, staying connected and your budget. **Part III** covers accommodation and arrival. **Part IV** covers university life: teaching, assessment, academic integrity, support and careers. **Part V** covers everyday money, part-time work, health and safety. **Part VI** covers life after your course: the Graduate Route and other options, going home, and the toolkit with checklists, a calendar and a directory.

### How each chapter works

Each chapter starts by telling you what it covers and what you should be able to do afterwards. It then gives the facts in plain language, step-by-step actions, tables for numbers, the mistakes students make most often, and three things to do next. Boxes are labelled in words so the book still works in black-and-white print: **Verify box, Example (invented), In plain words, Checklist, Watch out** and **Naara Insight**.

Short web addresses in this book point to official organisations. The full, up-to-date list of links is kept at naara.app/h/GBR-04, so nothing breaks when a web page moves.

Rules for the four UK nations differ on some points, such as housing and tuition for UK residents. The Student visa is a UK-wide system, but the international student levy applies to England only. Each chapter says when the nation matters.

## 48-hour quick-start checklist {.front}

Use this page if you already have an offer, or you are about to pay someone for help with admission or a visa. Each step is explained properly later in the book.

::: {.checklist title="If you have an offer and need to act this week"}
- Confirm the offer is real. Log in to the university's own applicant portal directly, not through a link in an email or message, and check that the university appears on the official GOV.UK register of student sponsors (Chapter 2).
- Read the offer conditions and the deadline to accept. Note the tuition deposit and its refund terms before you pay.
- Work out the money you must show for the visa: your first-year tuition (minus anything already paid) plus living costs for up to nine months (Chapter 3).
- Check the 28-day rule: the funds must be in an account that is allowed (yours, or your parents' or legal guardians' with the right proof) for 28 days in a row, and the 28 days must end within 31 days before you apply (Chapter 3).
- Check your English evidence. Most new applicants now need CEFR level B2 (Chapter 3).
- Ask the university when your CAS (Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies) will be issued and which documents it needs first.
- Ask whether your course needs an ATAS certificate before the CAS can be issued (Chapter 5).
:::

::: {.checklist title="Before you pay anyone for study help"}
- You know exactly who is being paid and for what, in writing.
- The agent is on the university's own list of authorised agents (check the university website).
- Nobody has promised admission, a visa or a scholarship for a fee. No one can lawfully promise that.
- Tuition is paid only to the university's official bank account, shown on the university's own website or portal.
- You will never be asked to hand over passwords to your visa or university account.
:::

::: {.plain title="Three rules before you spend money"}
First, confirm the university and the offer yourself, on the university's own website. Second, no one can guarantee a visa. Anyone who does is either lying or planning to commit fraud with your documents. Third, only show money that really belongs to you. Borrowed or temporary funds that are made to look like savings can lead to a refusal and a ban of up to ten years.
:::
