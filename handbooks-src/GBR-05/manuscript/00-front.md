## How to use this book {.front}

You do not have to read this book from cover to cover. Choose the path that matches your situation.

### By need

- **I land in the next few days and have nowhere to live:** read the 48-hour quick-start checklist, then Chapter 3 on what you can lawfully be asked to pay.
- **I am choosing a city or a type of home:** read Chapter 1 for the real market, then Part IV on neighbourhoods and housing types.
- **Someone is asking me for money, fast:** read Chapter 3, then Part III on scams, before you pay anything.
- **I already rent and have a problem:** read Chapter 2 on your rights, then Part V on repairs, rent rises and ending a tenancy.
- **I am a student:** read Chapter 12 and the companion handbook *Study in the UK* (GBR-04).

### By mood

- **I need answers fast:** read the "Do this next" box at the end of each chapter.
- **I want to understand properly:** read "What you need to know" and the mistakes list in each chapter.
- **I am anxious about money or scams:** read Chapter 3's cost tables and Part III. Most worry fades once you know the legal limits and the warning signs.

### Book map

**Part I** covers the market, your rights and what renting costs. **Part II** covers finding and securing a home, from searching and viewings to right-to-rent checks, guarantors and your agreement. **Part III** covers rental scams and unlawful landlord behaviour. **Part IV** covers where to live: regions and neighbourhoods, types of home, and the rules in Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland. **Part V** covers moving in, bills, repairs, rent rises, ending a tenancy and getting your deposit back. **Part VI** is the toolkit: calendar, checklists, letter templates, a directory and a glossary.

## 48-hour quick-start checklist {.front}

Use this page if you land soon and have no home yet, or if someone is pushing you to pay today. Each step is explained properly later in the book.

::: {.checklist title="If you land in the next few days"}
- Book a short stay (hotel, serviced flat or verified hostel) for the first week so you never have to rent in a panic.
- Decide your region and a monthly rent ceiling (Chapter 1), and check you can pass the income test or arrange a guarantor (Chapters 3 and 7).
- Prepare your paperwork in one folder: passport, visa or eVisa share-code method, proof of income or study, bank statements, references (Chapter 7).
- Set up a UK-capable phone and data before you land (Chapter 14), so you can take viewing calls.
- Never pay a deposit, holding fee or rent before you have seen the home or had it verified, and have the contract in writing (Chapters 8 and 9).
:::

::: {.checklist title="Before you pay anyone anything"}
- The payment is on the allowed list and within the legal limit for your nation (Chapter 3).
- You know who the landlord is, and the agent's company name and redress scheme (Chapter 9).
- The bank account name matches the landlord or the agent company.
- You will get a written receipt and the deposit scheme details within the legal time.
- You have photographed the home and the paperwork.
:::

::: {.plain title="Three rules"}
First, no one can lawfully demand months of rent up front in most private lets in England. Second, never pay for a home you have not seen or had verified by someone you trust. Third, keep every message and every receipt in one folder, because a record is your best protection.
:::
