# Living in your home {.part number="V" sub="Moving in without losing your deposit, paying the right bills, getting repairs done, handling rent rises, leaving properly and getting your money back."}

## Moving in: keys, inventory and the first week {.chapter number="14"}

This chapter walks you through move-in day: what to check, how to record the condition of the home, which accounts to open and who to tell. By the end you should have a dated record of the home's condition, working utilities and a list of the first-week tasks.

### What you need to know

The first day sets up the last day. The condition of the home when you move in decides what the landlord can claim when you leave. A careful inventory and a set of photographs protect your deposit better than any conversation later. In the same hour, you also start your utility and council tax responsibilities.

::: {.plain title="The golden rule of move-in day"}
If it is not photographed and sent in writing on day one, you may not be able to prove it later. Take photos, send them by email to the landlord or agent the same day, and keep the email.
:::

### Move-in day checklist

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| Documents | Check that you have the signed agreement, the deposit scheme details, the gas safety record, the EPC and the electrical safety report [VERIFY: documents the landlord must give you]{.v}. |
| Keys | Check that every key works, count them, and get a spare if the agreement allows. |
| Inventory | Compare the written inventory with the home, room by room, and note every difference. |
| Photographs | Photograph and video every room, the walls, floors, windows, appliances, meters and any damage, with date stamps. |
| Meters | Photograph the gas, electricity and water meter readings, with the meter numbers. |
| Safety | Test the smoke alarms and carbon monoxide alarms, and check you know where the stopcock and fuse box are. |
| Heating and hot water | Test them, and ask for the boiler's manual. |
| Contacts | Save the landlord's and agent's phone numbers and the emergency repair number. |
| Rubbish and recycling | Find the bin days and the council's recycling rules. |

![An electricity meter. Photograph the reading on move-in day, because a dated reading protects you from paying the previous tenant's energy.](images/GBR-05-c14-meter-reading.webp)

### The inventory in practice

An inventory is a written list of the home's contents and condition. Many landlords provide one. If they do not, write your own. Mark every difference in writing, return it within the time the agreement gives (often seven days), and keep a copy.

1. **Go room by room,** and write what is there and what condition it is in.
2. **Note existing damage:** marks, scuffs, stains, chips, loose handles, broken blinds, worn carpet.
3. **Take close-up photos** of each problem next to a coin or tape measure for scale.
4. **Take wide photos** of each wall and each corner.
5. **Test everything** that moves or switches on.
6. **Email the list and the photos** to the landlord and agent on day one, and ask for confirmation.

::: {.example title="The marks that were already there"}
A tenant moves into a flat in Sheffield and notices scuffs on the hallway wall, a cracked tile in the bathroom and a stain on the carpet. He photographs them, adds them to the inventory, and emails the landlord. Two years later, the landlord tries to deduct £250 for "damage" to the hallway and carpet. The tenant sends the dated email and photos, and the deposit scheme rules in his favour.
:::

### Set up your essentials in the first week

| Task | Why and how |
|---|---|
| Tell the council | Register for council tax from your move-in date, and apply for any discount or exemption (Chapter 15). |
| Set up energy | Ask the landlord who the current supplier is, give the supplier your meter readings and set up an account in your name. |
| Water | Find out which water company supplies the home and register. |
| Broadband and phone | Order early, because installation can take weeks. Use a data eSIM or mobile data until it is ready (Chapter 15). |
| TV licence | Buy one if you watch live TV or BBC iPlayer. |
| Contents insurance | Cover your belongings. The landlord's insurance does not cover them. |
| Register with a GP and dentist | Register where you now live. |
| Update your address | Tell your bank, employer, university, HMRC and the Home Office if your visa requires it [VERIFY: your visa's address-reporting duty]{.v}. |
| Mail redirection | Ask the previous tenant's mail not to be forwarded to you, and return it unopened. |
| Meet the neighbours | A short hello helps if there is ever a noise or access problem. |

### Meet your housemates and set the rules

In a house share, agree the basics early: who cleans what, bin duty, guests, noise at night, shared food and how to pay bills. A short, friendly written agreement avoids many arguments. Use a shared bills app or a joint account only if everyone is comfortable with it. Remember that on a joint tenancy, any one tenant's missed rent is everyone's problem.

::: {.checklist title="Move-in day"}
- I have the signed agreement, the deposit scheme details and the safety certificates.
- I have checked and photographed every room, appliance and meter.
- I have emailed the inventory and photos on day one.
- I have tested the smoke and CO alarms, the heating and the hot water.
- I know where the stopcock, fuse box and gas shut-off are.
- I have saved the repair and emergency numbers.
- I have told the council, the energy supplier and my bank my new address.
:::

### Mistakes that cost people money or time

- Not doing an inventory, or doing it a month later.
- Forgetting to photograph the meters.
- Putting energy bills in the landlord's or the previous tenant's name.
- Waiting weeks to register for council tax and then facing a backdated bill.
- Not checking the safety alarms.

:::

## Bills, council tax and keeping costs down {.chapter number="15"}

This chapter explains the bills a tenant pays, how to set them up, how to understand them and how to cut them. By the end you should be able to set up energy, water, council tax and broadband, and know which discounts and protections you can claim.

### What you need to know

Many first-time renters are surprised that rent is only part of the cost of a home. Energy, water, council tax, broadband, a TV licence and insurance together can add a third to the rent. Some of these may be included in your rent. Always ask.

| Bill | Who pays | Typical monthly range (one person) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Energy (gas and electricity) | Tenant, unless included | £100 to £160 | Ofgem's price cap for a typical household is about £1,723 a year from 1 October to 31 December 2026 [VERIFY: the cap for your period]{.v}. |
| Water and sewerage | Tenant, unless included | £30 to £60 | Depends on the water company and whether there is a meter. |
| Council tax | Tenant (liable), unless included or exempt | £0 to £200 | Band and council decide the bill; discounts and exemptions exist. |
| Broadband | Tenant | £25 to £40 | Often 12 to 24-month contracts. |
| Mobile phone | Tenant | £10 to £25 | SIM-only plans are cheapest. |
| TV licence | Tenant | About £15 | £180 a year, if you watch live TV or BBC iPlayer [VERIFY: current price]{.v}. |
| Contents insurance | Tenant | £5 to £15 | Covers your belongings. |

::: {.src}
Ofgem, council and consumer finance sources; planning estimates only, October 2026 (see Chapter 3).
:::

### Energy: setting up and saving

1. **Find the supplier.** Ask the landlord or agent, or use the meter's number to look up the supplier on the energy industry's meter-number service [VERIFY: the current look-up service]{.v}.
2. **Contact the supplier** on move-in day with your name, the date and the meter readings.
3. **Choose your tariff.** Many tenants are put on a "standard variable tariff", which follows the price cap. Compare fixed deals only if you plan to stay.
4. **Understand your meter.** A smart meter sends readings automatically. A prepayment meter needs top-ups. A traditional meter needs you to send readings.
5. **Save energy:** use a lower thermostat setting, bleed radiators, close curtains at night, wash at 30 degrees and turn off standby.
6. **Get help if you struggle.** Suppliers must offer support, and you may qualify for help such as the Warm Home Discount [VERIFY: current eligibility]{.v}. Join the Priority Services Register if you are vulnerable.

![A smart-meter display showing the cost per hour. Photograph the meter reading when you move in and when you move out.](images/GBR-05-c15-smart-meter.webp)

### Council tax

Council tax is a local tax paid by the people who live in a home. It depends on the property's valuation band (A to H in England and Scotland) and the council. In England, the average Band D bill in 2026/27 is about £2,392 a year [VERIFY]{.v}.

| Situation | What happens |
|---|---|
| Full-time students | Exempt from council tax; apply with a certificate from the university. |
| A single adult in the home | A 25 per cent discount. |
| A home occupied only by full-time students | Exempt. |
| Low income | You may get help through a Council Tax Reduction scheme [VERIFY: your council's scheme]{.v}. |
| Bills included in the rent | Your landlord pays; check in writing. |

Register with the council from your move-in date and ask what discounts apply. Pay by direct debit, in ten or twelve instalments, and never ignore a council tax letter, because arrears can lead to court action.

### Water, broadband and insurance

- **Water.** Your water company depends on where you live. Some homes have a meter, and others are charged by property value. Ask for a meter if you live alone and use little water.
- **Broadband.** Check coverage before you move in. Ask the landlord whether you may install a line or drill, and book early. If you are moving in during winter, installation can take several weeks.
- **Contents insurance.** Choose cover for your laptop, phone and clothes. Check whether the policy covers accidental damage and theft away from home.

### Avoiding unnecessary costs

| Cost trap | What to do |
|---|---|
| Out-of-contract broadband and energy deals | Check renewal dates, and compare before the end. |
| Standing charges and early-exit fees | Read the fine print before you sign. |
| Late payment fees | Use direct debits, but keep enough in your account. |
| Paying for a service you do not need (TV package, extended warranty) | Ask for the cheapest plan that does the job. |
| Sharing bills badly | Use a written split. |

### Step by step: set up your bills

1. **Ask which bills are included** and write it down.
2. **Register for council tax** within a week.
3. **Set up energy** with a supplier, with your meter readings.
4. **Register with the water company.**
5. **Order broadband** and, if needed, a TV licence.
6. **Insure your belongings.**
7. **Put the due dates in your calendar** and set up direct debits.

::: {.example title="The bills behind a £620 room"}
A tenant in a house share in Leeds pays £620 a month rent. Energy and water are split four ways at £45 a month each, broadband is £10 each, and his council tax is zero because he is a full-time student. His phone costs £12 and his contents insurance £6. His real monthly cost for the room is £620 plus £73 = £693. He builds a monthly budget around the real figure.
:::

### Mistakes that cost people money or time

- Not registering for council tax and receiving a backdated bill.
- Leaving the energy on the previous tenant's account.
- Not sending meter readings and being billed on estimates.
- Missing a bill and damaging your credit record.
- Assuming that bills are included when they are not.

::: {.verify}
Check before you act: Ofgem's current price cap, your council's council tax and discount pages, your water company and the TV Licensing website. Last verified: 19 October 2026.
:::

::: {.next}
- Ask your landlord which bills are included, in writing.
- Register for council tax and energy on move-in day.
- Set up direct debits and calendar reminders.
:::

## Repairs, safety and damp {.chapter number="16"}

This chapter explains what a landlord must repair, how to ask in a way that works, the safety checks a home must have and what to do about damp and mould. By the end you should be able to write an effective repair request and escalate it if nothing happens.

### What you need to know

A landlord in England is responsible for the structure and exterior of the home, the water, gas and electrical installations, heating and hot water, and the sanitary fittings, such as the bath, basin and toilet. The home must also be fit for people to live in. You are responsible for looking after the home in a reasonable way and for reporting problems promptly. These rules differ in Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland (Chapter 13).

### What must the landlord provide and check?

| Item | The rule (England) | What to ask for |
|---|---|---|
| Gas safety | A gas safety check each year by a Gas Safe registered engineer; a copy must be given to you before you move in, or within 28 days of the check [VERIFY]{.v} | A copy of the current gas safety record. |
| Electrical safety | An electrical installation condition report (EICR) at least every five years | A copy of the current report. |
| Smoke alarms | At least one on each storey used as living accommodation | Test them on move-in day. |
| Carbon monoxide alarms | In any room with a fixed combustion appliance, such as a boiler or a wood burner [VERIFY: current regulations]{.v} | Check they work. |
| Energy Performance Certificate | A valid certificate with a minimum rating of E in most cases | A copy of the EPC. |
| Fitness for human habitation | The home must be safe, healthy and free from serious hazards | Report problems in writing. |
| Fire safety | Fire doors, escape routes, extinguishers and alarms in shared houses and larger buildings | Ask for the fire risk assessment if relevant. |

::: {.src}
Compiled from GOV.UK landlord and tenant guidance, October 2026. Requirements differ in Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland, and some are changing.
:::

![A gas boiler on a kitchen wall. A current gas safety record and working smoke and carbon monoxide alarms are not optional.](images/GBR-05-c16-boiler-alarm.webp)

### How to report a repair, step by step

1. **Tell the landlord or agent in writing,** by email or through the agent's portal. A phone call is not enough as evidence.
2. **Describe the problem clearly:** what, where, when it started and how it affects you.
3. **Attach photos or a short video** with a date stamp.
4. **Say what you want and by when.** For example: "Please arrange a repair within 14 days. This is urgent, because the heating is not working."
5. **Keep a record** of your messages, the replies and dates.
6. **Allow access** at reasonable times when the landlord books a visit, with proper notice.
7. **Follow up** after a week, then after the stated deadline.
8. **Escalate** to the council if nothing happens (see below).

### Urgent or not?

| Problem | Urgency | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Gas smell | Emergency | Leave the building, call the National Gas Emergency Service on 0800 111 999 [VERIFY: number]{.v}, do not use electrics or flames. |
| No heating or hot water in winter | Urgent | Report at once and ask for a repair within days. |
| Burst pipe, flooding or no water | Urgent | Turn off the stopcock and call the landlord or emergency contact. |
| No electricity, exposed wiring or sparking | Urgent | Turn off the supply if safe and call the landlord. |
| Broken lock or window on the ground floor | Urgent for security | Report immediately and ask for a temporary fix. |
| Mould or damp | Prompt | Report in writing with photos and ask for an inspection. |
| Dripping tap, loose handle, scuffed paint | Routine | Report and allow a few weeks. |

### Damp and mould

Damp and mould are common in UK homes and can harm your health, especially for children and people with asthma. There are three main causes: **condensation** (moist air from cooking, drying and breathing meets cold walls), **penetrating damp** (water coming in through the roof, walls or pipes) and **rising damp** (water moving up from the ground). The landlord is responsible for fixing structural causes. You can reduce condensation by ventilating, using extractor fans and not drying clothes on radiators.

1. **Photograph the problem** and when it began.
2. **Report it in writing** and ask for an inspection and a repair plan.
3. **Wipe small areas** of mould with a mould spray and ventilate the room.
4. **Do not paint over it.**
5. **Escalate** if nothing happens: ask the council's environmental health or private-sector housing team to inspect.
6. **Know the changing rules.** The Renters' Rights Act brings a Decent Homes Standard and Awaab's Law-style timescales to private homes in England in later phases, with dates still to be confirmed [VERIFY: current status of Awaab's Law and the Decent Homes Standard for private renting]{.v}.

::: {.warn}
Do not stop paying rent because repairs are not done. Withholding rent can give a landlord a ground to evict you, and may harm your credit record. Instead, report in writing, escalate to the council and take advice from Shelter or Citizens Advice.
:::

### If the landlord does not act

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| 1 | Send a reminder, citing the date of your first report and a clear deadline. |
| 2 | Contact the agent's complaints process and the redress scheme if it is an agent. |
| 3 | Contact your council's environmental health or private housing team to inspect. The council can order the landlord to fix serious hazards. |
| 4 | Take advice from Shelter, Citizens Advice or a law centre about options such as a court order for repairs or compensation. |
| 5 | Keep the paper trail: all messages, photographs and letters. |

Councils have powers to inspect homes and to serve improvement notices. A landlord who ignores them can be fined. Tenants may also be able to claim compensation for the effect of disrepair, with legal advice [VERIFY: options and time limits]{.v}.

::: {.example title="A repair that worked"}
A tenant in Birmingham reports that her boiler has stopped working in November. She emails the agent with a photo of the error code and writes: "This is urgent because there is no heating or hot water. Please arrange an engineer within three days." Nothing happens for five days, so she sends a reminder and contacts the council. An engineer visits two days later, replaces a part and gives her a gas safety certificate. She keeps the emails in a folder.
:::

### Your duties as a tenant

You must use the home properly, report problems quickly, keep it reasonably clean, avoid damage, allow access for repairs and inspections with notice, and not make changes without permission. Small things, such as changing light bulbs, using the fuse box, clearing blocked drains from your own waste and keeping the garden tidy if the agreement says so, may be your responsibility.

### Mistakes that cost people money or time

- Reporting by phone only.
- Waiting until a small problem becomes a large one.
- Not allowing access for repairs, which weakens your position.
- Withholding rent.
- Painting over mould.

::: {.verify}
Check before you act: GOV.UK on repairs, health and safety in rented homes, your council's housing team, the Gas Safe register, Shelter and Citizens Advice. Last verified: 19 October 2026.
:::

## Rent rises, staying on and leaving {.chapter number="17"}

This chapter explains how rent can rise, how to challenge it, how to end your tenancy properly, what happens if a landlord wants you to leave and how to avoid costs when you go. By the end you should be able to handle a rent rise letter, give valid notice and leave with your deposit intact.

### What you need to know

Since 1 May 2026, most private tenancies in England are periodic. They have no fixed end date. This changes both rent rises and leaving: your landlord can no longer simply decline to renew, and rents can rise only through a formal process. You can leave with two months' notice.

### Rent rises

| Rule | What it means |
|---|---|
| Once a year | A landlord can propose a rent increase no more than once every 12 months. |
| Not in the first 12 months | The first year of a new tenancy is protected. |
| Proper notice | The landlord must serve a Section 13 notice on Form 4A, giving at least two months' notice. |
| Market rent | A rise above the market rent can be challenged at the First-tier Tribunal. |
| No other route | Rent review clauses in the agreement no longer let the landlord raise the rent without the formal process. |

::: {.src}
GOV.UK Renters' Rights Act overview for tenants, October 2026.
:::

### If you receive a rent increase notice

1. **Check the notice.** Is it on the correct form (Form 4A)? Does it give at least two months' notice? Is the rent increase in the first 12 months? If not, it may be invalid.
2. **Compare the new rent** with similar homes nearby on listing sites. Keep screenshots.
3. **Decide:** accept, negotiate, challenge or leave.
4. **To negotiate,** write politely, show your evidence and offer a compromise.
5. **To challenge,** apply to the First-tier Tribunal (Property Chamber) before the new rent starts [VERIFY: the deadline and the fee, which is often free for tenants]{.v}. The tribunal decides the market rent, and the rent cannot rise above what the landlord proposed.
6. **Keep paying the current rent** until the new rent is lawfully due.

::: {.example title="Challenging a rise with evidence"}
A tenant in Bristol receives notice that his rent will rise from £1,100 to £1,350. He finds four similar flats at £1,120 to £1,200 on listing sites, takes screenshots and writes to the landlord proposing £1,180. The landlord agrees to £1,200. If not, he was ready to apply to the tribunal before the increase date with his evidence.
:::

![Loading a rental truck. Give notice in writing and plan the move-out a few weeks ahead.](images/GBR-05-c17-moving-boxes.webp)

### Ending your tenancy

To leave a periodic tenancy in England, you give two months' notice in writing, ending on a rent-due date, unless you and the landlord agree otherwise in writing. You remain liable for rent throughout the notice period. In a joint tenancy, check how the notice of one tenant affects the others [VERIFY: the rule for joint tenants]{.v}.

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| 1 | Check your agreement and the official guidance on notice. |
| 2 | Write your notice with the date, the address, your name and the date the tenancy will end. |
| 3 | Send it in a way you can prove (email and recorded delivery) and keep copies. |
| 4 | Agree a check-out date, and ask for the check-out process in writing. |
| 5 | Arrange for the meter readings, the final bills and the redirection of your post. |
| 6 | Clean the home, return the keys and take photos. |

### If the landlord wants you to leave

A landlord must now rely on a legal ground set out in Section 8 of the Housing Act 1988, and use the correct notice. Notice is usually four months for grounds such as selling the home or moving in, and these grounds cannot be used in the first 12 months of the tenancy. Different grounds apply for rent arrears, anti-social behaviour and some student houses [VERIFY: grounds and notice periods]{.v}. If you do not leave, the landlord must apply to court. Do not leave before a court order without advice.

| If the landlord says | What to check |
|---|---|
| "I am selling" | Is it after the first 12 months? Is the notice correct, and four months long? |
| "I am moving in" or "a family member is moving in" | Is it after the first 12 months, and is the notice proper? |
| "You owe rent" | Have you missed the payments they say? Can you pay and get advice? |
| "You must leave now" | A request is not a legal order. Get advice from Shelter. |

### Moving out well

1. **Finish your tasks:** pay all rent and bills, cancel direct debits and contracts.
2. **Take final meter readings** on the day you leave and send them to your suppliers and the landlord.
3. **Clean to the standard of the move-in inventory.**
4. **Return all keys** and ask for a signed receipt.
5. **Photograph every room,** and the cleaned appliances and meters, with date stamps.
6. **Leave a forwarding address** with the landlord, the council, your bank and the deposit scheme.
7. **Cancel or transfer** your broadband, TV licence and insurance.
8. **Redirect your post.**

::: {.checklist title="Before I give notice"}
- I have read my agreement and the official notice rules.
- My notice is in writing, correctly dated and sent in a provable way.
- I know how much rent I owe up to the end date.
- I have arranged final bills, meter readings and cancellations.
- I have a plan for where I will live next.
:::

### Mistakes that cost people money or time

- Giving notice verbally.
- Not checking the form and dates on a rent increase.
- Leaving on a landlord's request without a court order or advice.
- Forgetting to cancel broadband and direct debits.
- Giving notice before you have somewhere else to go.

::: {.verify}
Check before you act: GOV.UK on rent increases and ending a tenancy, the First-tier Tribunal's guidance for tenants, your agreement and Shelter's guidance on notice. Last verified: 19 October 2026.
:::

## Getting your deposit back {.chapter number="18"}

This chapter explains how deposits are protected, what a landlord may deduct, how check-out works, and how to dispute a deduction. By the end you should be able to leave a home with a strong case for a full refund, and know the steps if the landlord disagrees.

### What you need to know

Your deposit is your money, held as security. In England and Wales it must be placed in a government-approved scheme within 30 days, and you must be given the scheme's details. At the end of the tenancy, the landlord can claim only for losses the tenancy allows, such as unpaid rent, unpaid bills and damage beyond fair wear and tear. The rest is yours.

::: {.plain title="Fair wear and tear versus damage"}
Fair wear and tear is the normal ageing of a home that comes from people living in it. Damage is something beyond that, such as a burn, a broken door or a large stain. A landlord cannot deduct for fair wear and tear, or to improve the home ("betterment"), for example by replacing a five-year-old carpet with a new one.
:::

![A small house and keys held in open hands. Photograph the clean home and get a receipt for the keys when you hand them back.](images/GBR-05-c18-returning-keys.webp)

### Where your deposit is protected

| Scheme | England and Wales | Scotland | Northern Ireland |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deposit Protection Service | Yes | No | No |
| mydeposits | Yes | Yes (mydeposits Scotland) | Yes (mydeposits Northern Ireland) |
| Tenancy Deposit Scheme | Yes | No | Yes (TDS Northern Ireland) |
| Others | | SafeDeposits Scotland, Letting Protection Service Scotland | |

::: {.src}
Compiled from scheme websites and government guidance, October 2026. Confirm the approved schemes for your nation.
:::

In England, a landlord who does not protect a deposit and give you the prescribed information within 30 days can be ordered to repay between one and three times the deposit [VERIFY: current penalty and time limits]{.v}. Check your scheme's certificate and register your details on the scheme's website.

### Getting ready for check-out

1. **Compare the home with the move-in inventory** and photos.
2. **Clean thoroughly:** oven, fridge, bathroom, floors, windows, skirting boards and appliances. A professional clean is not required unless the home was professionally cleaned at the start and the agreement requires it.
3. **Repair small things you caused:** a nail hole, a broken handle or a chipped paint mark.
4. **Remove all your belongings and rubbish.**
5. **Record the meter readings.**
6. **Photograph every room** at the end, with date stamps.
7. **Attend the check-out** if there is one, and ask for a copy of the report.
8. **Return the keys** and ask for a receipt.

### What happens next

| Event | What should happen |
|---|---|
| You and the landlord agree the amount | The scheme releases the deposit, usually within about ten days [VERIFY: scheme timescale]{.v}. |
| The landlord claims a deduction and you agree | The agreed amount is paid to the landlord and the rest to you. |
| You disagree | You can raise a dispute with the scheme, which offers a free independent adjudication using the evidence both sides provide. |
| The landlord does not respond | The scheme can release your deposit if the landlord fails to engage, within the scheme's rules. |

### Winning a dispute: the evidence that counts

| Evidence | How it helps |
|---|---|
| Dated move-in inventory and photos | Shows the starting condition. |
| Dated check-out photos and video | Shows the end condition. |
| Inventory comparisons | Shows what changed and what is wear and tear. |
| Receipts for cleaning and repairs | Shows what you did. |
| Messages with the landlord | Shows what was said and agreed. |
| The age and condition of the item | Helps the adjudicator decide a fair deduction. |

An adjudicator looks at the evidence, not at who shouts louder. Landlords must prove the loss and show that the deduction is fair. Submit your evidence in a clear folder, with a short, polite explanation.

::: {.example title="Disputing a cleaning charge"}
A tenant moves out of a flat in Glasgow and receives a claim for £180 for professional cleaning and £220 to replace the carpet. He submits dated check-in photos showing the stains that were already there, check-out photos showing the clean flat, and a receipt for the cleaning he did himself. The carpet was nine years old. The adjudicator awards the landlord £30 for a small stain and returns the rest.
:::

### If your deposit is not protected

Ask your landlord in writing which scheme holds the deposit. If none does, you can contact the three English schemes to check, and then take advice from Shelter, Citizens Advice or a solicitor about claiming compensation of up to three times the deposit [VERIFY]{.v}. Do this even after you have moved out, because time limits apply.

### Mistakes that cost people money or time

- Not keeping check-in and check-out photos.
- Cleaning only the visible parts.
- Leaving without giving a forwarding address.
- Agreeing to unfair deductions without checking the evidence.
- Missing the dispute deadline.

::: {.verify}
Check before you act: your deposit scheme's guidance on disputes and timescales, the deposit protection rules for your nation and Shelter's advice on deposits. Last verified: 19 October 2026.
:::
