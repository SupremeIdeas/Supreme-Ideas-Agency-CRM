# Toolkit {.part number="VI" sub="A calendar, checklists, letter templates, a directory of official help and a glossary you can print or keep on your phone."}

## The toolkit {.chapter number="19"}

This chapter collects the tools you will use again and again: a timeline from search to move-out, one-page checklists, templates for the letters that matter, a directory of official and free help, and a glossary. Print it, or save it on your phone.

### Housing calendar: from decision to deposit

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| 8 to 12 weeks before you move | Choose your region and budget (Chapters 1 and 11). Check you can pass the income test or arrange a guarantor (Chapter 7). Set up your UKVI account and test a share code. |
| 6 to 8 weeks before | Set up saved searches and alerts (Chapter 4). Book a short stay for the first week. Prepare your evidence pack (Chapter 6). |
| 2 to 4 weeks before | Contact agents, and arrange viewings for the days after you land. Do not pay anything before viewing (Chapter 5). |
| Week 1 after landing | View in person, apply for your chosen home, pay a holding deposit only against a written receipt (Chapter 6). |
| Week 2 to 3 | Referencing, tenancy agreement, signing. Pay the deposit and first rent by bank transfer to a verified account (Chapters 3 and 8). |
| Move-in day | Inventory, photos, meter readings, safety checks (Chapter 14). |
| First week | Council tax, energy, water, broadband, insurance, GP (Chapters 14 and 15). |
| During the tenancy | Report repairs in writing (Chapter 16), keep records, keep paying on time. |
| Rent rise notice | Check the form and dates (Chapter 17). |
| Two months before leaving | Give written notice, plan the move-out, arrange cleaning and final bills (Chapter 17). |
| Move-out day | Photos, meter readings, return keys, forwarding address (Chapter 18). |
| Within weeks | Deposit returned, or raise a dispute with the scheme (Chapter 18). |

### One-page master checklists

::: {.checklist title="Before I pay any money"}
- I have seen the home or someone I trust has.
- I know who the landlord is, and I have verified the agent's registration.
- The payment type is on the allowed list and within the legal limit for my nation.
- The bank account name matches the landlord or the agent company.
- I have the contract, or the holding-deposit terms, in writing.
- I will get a written receipt.
:::

::: {.checklist title="Before I sign the tenancy agreement"}
- I have read every page and schedule.
- I have the gas safety record, EICR, EPC and the government's tenant guide.
- I know which deposit scheme will protect my deposit.
- I know who is liable for the rent on a joint tenancy.
- I have photographed or scanned every page I signed.
:::

::: {.checklist title="Move-in week"}
- I have emailed the inventory and photos on day one.
- I have recorded the meter readings.
- I have registered for council tax and set up energy and water.
- I have tested the alarms and know the shut-off points.
- I have saved the repair and emergency numbers.
:::

::: {.checklist title="Moving out"}
- I gave written notice that ends on a rent-due date.
- I have paid all rent and bills and recorded the final readings.
- I cleaned to the move-in standard and photographed every room.
- I returned the keys and kept a receipt.
- I gave a forwarding address to the landlord, the council and the deposit scheme.
:::

### Letter and email templates

**1. Asking in writing which payments are lawful (England)**

> Dear (name),
> Thank you for your message about (address). Before I pay, please confirm in writing the amount of the holding deposit, the tenancy deposit, the first month's rent, the date by which you will give me a decision, the deposit scheme you will use, and whether any other payment is required and on what legal basis. I understand that, in England, the allowed payments are limited by the Tenant Fees Act 2019 and the Renters' Rights Act 2025.
> Kind regards, (name)

**2. Repair request**

> Subject: Repair needed at (address): (problem)
> Dear (name),
> I am writing to report (describe the problem, where, and since when). It affects (heating, safety, health, security). I have attached photos dated (date). Please arrange a repair by (date), or let me know when an engineer can attend. I will allow access on (days and times) with 24 hours' notice. If this is not resolved, I may contact the council's housing team.
> Kind regards, (name, address, phone)

**3. Challenging a rent increase**

> Dear (name),
> I received your notice dated (date) proposing a rent of £(amount) from (date). I believe this is above the market rent for similar homes nearby, and I attach examples: (list with links or screenshots). I propose £(amount). If we cannot agree, I will consider applying to the First-tier Tribunal before the new rent starts.
> Kind regards, (name)

**4. Notice to end a periodic tenancy (England)**

> Dear (name),
> I am giving you two months' notice to end my tenancy of (address). The tenancy will end on (date, which is a rent-due date at least two months from the date of this letter). I will arrange the check-out, return the keys and give you a forwarding address. Please confirm receipt and the check-out date.
> Kind regards, (name, date)

**5. Asking the deposit scheme for the return of my deposit**

> Dear (scheme),
> My tenancy at (address) ended on (date) and I left on (date). My deposit of £(amount) was registered under reference (number). I have not received the deposit or a claim from my landlord within the scheme's time limit. Please tell me the next steps. My forwarding address and bank details are (details).
> Kind regards, (name)

### Directory: official and free help

| Topic | Organisation | What it gives you |
|---|---|---|
| Housing rights and emergency advice | Shelter (England), Shelter Cymru, Shelter Scotland, Housing Rights (Northern Ireland) | Free advice, helplines and guides. |
| General rights and consumer problems | Citizens Advice, and Citizens Advice Scotland | Free advice, including on scams and deposits. |
| Rules and guides | GOV.UK, GOV.WALES, mygov.scot, NI Direct | Official guides for tenants and landlords. |
| Repairs and unsafe homes | Your local council housing, environmental health or private-sector housing team | Inspections and enforcement. |
| Fraud | Action Fraud, Police Scotland | Reporting scams. |
| Deposit protection | Deposit Protection Service, mydeposits, Tenancy Deposit Scheme, and the Scottish and NI schemes | Protection, advice and free dispute resolution. |
| Agent complaints | The Property Ombudsman, Property Redress Scheme | Redress if an agent fails you. |
| Tribunal | First-tier Tribunal (Property Chamber) | Rent challenges and some disputes. |
| Immigration status and share codes | UK Visas and Immigration (UKVI) | Your eVisa and share code. |
| Energy | Ofgem, your supplier, Citizens Advice | Price cap, complaints and help with bills. |
| Equality and discrimination | Equality Advisory Support Service | Advice on discrimination. |

### Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| AST | Assured shorthold tenancy, the old English private tenancy; most became periodic on 1 May 2026. |
| Assured periodic tenancy | A tenancy that rolls on until ended lawfully. |
| Breach | Breaking a term of the agreement. |
| Client money protection | A scheme that protects tenants' and landlords' money held by an agent. |
| Contract-holder | The Welsh term for a tenant. |
| Council tax | A local tax paid by the people who live in a home. |
| Deposit protection scheme | An approved scheme that holds your deposit. |
| EICR | Electrical installation condition report. |
| EPC | Energy Performance Certificate. |
| Fair wear and tear | Normal ageing of a home. |
| Form 4A | The form for a rent increase notice in England. |
| Guarantor | A person or company who promises to pay your rent if you cannot. |
| HMO | House in multiple occupation. |
| Holding deposit | Money paid to reserve a home while checks are done, capped at one week's rent in England. |
| Inventory | A list of the home's contents and condition. |
| Joint and several liability | Each tenant on a joint agreement is responsible for the whole rent. |
| Right to rent | The check that a person can lawfully rent in England. |
| Section 8 | The route for a landlord to seek possession on a legal ground. |
| Share code | A code that proves your immigration status to a landlord or employer. |
| Tenant Fees Act 2019 | The law limiting what landlords and agents can charge in England. |
