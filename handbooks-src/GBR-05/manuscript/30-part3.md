# Scams and unlawful landlord behaviour {.part number="III" sub="The tricks that cost newcomers thousands, how to check who you are dealing with, and what to do when a landlord breaks the law."}

## Rental scams: how they work and how to beat them {.chapter number="9"}

This chapter explains the rental scams that target newcomers, the checks that expose them, how to pay safely and what to do if you have already paid. By the end you should be able to verify a landlord or agent in ten minutes and know where to report a fraud.

### What you need to know

Rental fraud is common because it works on three pressures: you need a home fast, you are far away and good homes really do disappear in hours. Scammers copy real photos, offer a rent that is slightly below the market and push you to pay before you see the home. The good news is that almost every scam fails at the same few checks.

::: {.plain title="The pattern behind most scams"}
A low price, a landlord or agent who cannot meet, an urgent deadline and a request to pay money before you have seen the home or signed anything. If you see two or more of these together, stop.
:::

### The common scams

| Scam | How it works | The check that exposes it |
|---|---|---|
| Phantom listing | A home that does not exist or is not for rent is advertised with copied photos | Search the photos and the address; view in person; check ownership. |
| Cloned advert | A real agent's listing is copied with a new phone number or email | Find the agent's real website and call the number there. |
| "Landlord abroad" | The owner is "overseas" and will send the keys by post after you pay a deposit | Never pay for a home you have not seen or had verified. |
| Fake agent | A person pretends to work for a real agency | Check the agent's registration, address and the branch's phone number independently. |
| Double letting | The same home is let to several people, and each pays a deposit | A viewing with the real owner or agent and a signed agreement before you pay. |
| Sub-letting scam | A tenant lets out a home they do not have the right to sublet | Ask for the landlord's permission and the head tenancy; check the landlord's identity. |
| Fake fees | Fees for referencing, "right to rent", admin or "priority" | Check the allowed list (Chapter 3). |
| Payment diversion | After you agree, an email changes the bank details | Phone the agent on a trusted number before paying to new details. |
| Overpayment refund | You are sent too much money and asked to send the "extra" back | Do not return money; ring your bank. |
| Fake utility, council tax or TV licence calls | A caller demands payment or threatens you | Hang up and call the organisation on its official number. |

Compiled from Action Fraud, Citizens Advice, Shelter and agent-industry guidance, October 2026.

### The ten-minute check

1. **Reverse-search the photos.** Use an image search to see whether they appear on other adverts or on a sale listing.
2. **Compare the rent with similar homes nearby.** A rent far below the market is a warning sign.
3. **Find the agent's own website** and call the office number listed there. Do not use a number sent in a message.
4. **Check the agent's registration.** Letting agents in England must belong to a government-approved redress scheme and, in practice, should hold client money protection [VERIFY: current legal requirements]{.v}. A reputable agent also belongs to a professional body such as Propertymark or the National Residential Landlords Association [VERIFY: current names]{.v}.
5. **Check the landlord.** For a private landlord, compare the name with the Land Registry title register for the address (a small fee), and ask for photo ID [VERIFY: how to search the register]{.v}.
6. **Check the company.** Search Companies House for the agent's company name and address.
7. **View the home in person,** with the agent or the owner, who must have keys.
8. **Match the names.** The name on the agreement, the account and the owner should match.
9. **Get the contract in writing** before you pay.
10. **Pay by bank transfer** to a verified account, and keep the receipt.

### How to pay safely

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Pay by bank transfer to the agent's company account or the named landlord | Pay in cash, with gift cards, by cryptocurrency or to a personal account that does not match |
| Check the account name and use the bank's "confirmation of payee" prompt | Ignore a name mismatch warning |
| Pay the holding deposit only after viewing and a written receipt | Pay "to hold" a home before a viewing |
| Phone the agent on a trusted number if the bank details change | Trust an email with new bank details |
| Keep every receipt, message and the advert (screenshots) | Delete evidence when you feel embarrassed |

::: {.warn}
Never pay a deposit or rent through a payment app to a person, by international money transfer, or to someone who says they are abroad. These routes are hard to reverse and are favourite routes for fraud.
:::

### If you have paid a scammer

1. **Call your bank at once** on the number on your card or in your banking app. Ask to report an authorised push payment (APP) scam. Banks must reimburse many victims of this kind of fraud, subject to limits and conditions, if you report it promptly [VERIFY: current reimbursement rules and the time limit for claims]{.v}.
2. **Report it to Action Fraud** (in England, Wales and Northern Ireland, at actionfraud.police.uk or on 0300 123 2040), or to Police Scotland on 101 [VERIFY: current contact details]{.v}.
3. **Report the advert** to the website where you found it.
4. **Keep the evidence:** messages, receipts, the advert, the phone number and the bank details.
5. **Warn your community,** including your university, employer or faith group.
6. **Get emotional support.** Many victims feel ashamed, but these frauds are organised and sophisticated. Reporting helps others.

::: {.example title="The payment that was stopped"}
A student receives a listing on social media for a room at £350 a month in Birmingham, with a message from a "landlord in Dubai" asking for a £700 deposit and the first month's rent to "reserve" it. She searches the photos and finds that they are from a sale listing in another city. She refuses to pay and reports the advert. A friend who had already paid £600 to a similar listing phones his bank within the hour and the bank freezes the receiving account and starts a recovery.
:::

### Scams that follow you after you move in

- **Fake agents or landlords** who contact you later and ask for money for "repairs" or "administration".
- **Fake council tax, TV licence or energy company calls and emails.**
- **Fake deposit-scheme messages** that ask you to click a link to "claim" your deposit.
- **Fake right-to-rent or Home Office messages** that ask for money to "renew" your status.

Hang up, do not click, and contact the organisation yourself using the details on its official website.

::: {.checklist title="Scam check before I pay"}
- I have seen the home or had it checked by someone I trust.
- I have verified the agent's registration or the landlord's identity.
- The names on the agreement, the bank account and the ownership records match.
- The payment type is lawful and within the limit (Chapter 3).
- I am paying by bank transfer and I will get a written receipt.
- Nobody is rushing me.
:::

### Mistakes that cost people money or time

- Paying because the home looked perfect and the price looked fair.
- Believing a story about the landlord being abroad.
- Trusting a phone number or email in a message, not on the agent's official site.
- Paying to a person's account because "the company account is being changed".
- Staying silent because of embarrassment.

::: {.verify}
Check before you act: the agent's redress scheme and client money protection, Companies House, the Land Registry, Action Fraud's current advice and your bank's APP-fraud policy. Last verified: 19 October 2026.
:::

## When a landlord breaks the law {.chapter number="10"}

This chapter explains what a landlord may and may not do once you are a tenant: entering your home, ending your tenancy, cutting off services, charging illegal fees, retaliating when you complain and discriminating against you. By the end you should be able to recognise unlawful behaviour and know exactly who to call.

### What you need to know

Most landlords are fair, and most problems are mistakes, not malice. But some landlords test whether a new arrival knows their rights. In England and Wales, unlawful eviction and harassment of a tenant are criminal offences. Your rights do not depend on your nationality, visa or how long you have lived in the UK.

### What a landlord may not do

| Behaviour | The rule (England) | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Enter without notice | The landlord must give at least 24 hours' written notice and visit at a reasonable time, except in a genuine emergency | Write to them citing the clause and the law; refuse an unannounced entry politely. |
| Change the locks or put your belongings out | An unlawful eviction, a criminal offence | Call the police and the council's housing team; contact Shelter. |
| Cut off gas, electricity, water or heating | Harassment and a criminal offence | Report it to the council; call your supplier. |
| Evict without a court order | The landlord must use the legal process; Section 21 no longer exists, and Section 8 needs a ground and, if you stay, a court order | Do not leave on request; seek advice at once. |
| Threaten you, shout at you or intimidate you | Harassment | Keep a diary and evidence; report to the council. |
| Threaten to report you to immigration | An attempt to intimidate; the landlord cannot end your tenancy by doing so | Take advice from Shelter or a regulated adviser; keep evidence. |
| Evict because you complained about repairs | Retaliatory eviction is restricted [VERIFY: current protections after 1 May 2026]{.v} | Put complaints in writing; contact the council. |
| Take a deposit and not protect it | In England, deposits must be protected within 30 days | Ask for the scheme details; the landlord can face a penalty of one to three times the deposit [VERIFY]{.v}. |
| Charge banned fees | Unlawful under the Tenant Fees Act 2019 | Ask for a refund in writing; report to trading standards. |
| Refuse you because of children, benefits, race, religion or disability | Unlawful discrimination | Keep evidence; contact Citizens Advice, the Equality Advisory Support Service or the council. |
| Take rent before you sign or over one month in advance | Unlawful in most private lets in England since 1 May 2026 | Refuse; report to the council. |

### If you are told to leave

1. **Do not panic and do not move out immediately.** A landlord cannot lawfully make you leave without following the legal process.
2. **Ask for the notice in writing** and check it. Is it on the right form? Does it state a legal ground? Is the notice period long enough?
3. **Keep paying rent** while you get advice. Stopping gives the landlord a ground to evict.
4. **Call Shelter** (their helpline is free) or Citizens Advice, or ask your council's housing team [VERIFY: contact details]{.v}.
5. **Go to the court hearing,** if the landlord applies for a possession order. Courts can give you more time, and the council may have a duty to help if you risk becoming homeless.
6. **Do not accept a request to sign a "voluntary surrender"** or a "mutual termination" without advice.

### If the landlord locks you out or cuts off services

1. **Call the police on 999 if you are in danger,** or 101 for a non-emergency.
2. **Call the council's housing or "tenancy relations" team,** which can act against a landlord.
3. **Contact Shelter** for emergency advice and, if needed, a solicitor for an emergency court order.
4. **Document everything:** photos, times, messages, witness names.

### Keeping evidence

| Keep | How |
|---|---|
| The agreement and every document you signed | Scan and back up. |
| Rent receipts and bank transfers | Download bank statements monthly. |
| Messages and emails with the landlord | Take screenshots and keep them in one folder. |
| Photos and videos of the home and any problems | Date-stamp them. |
| A diary of incidents | Date, time, what happened and who was there. |
| Letters from the council, deposit scheme or tribunal | Keep originals. |

### If your landlord says you have "no rights"

Some landlords tell newcomers that they have no rights because of their immigration status or because the tenancy was "informal". This is false. Most tenants have rights, even without a written agreement. If you are unsure, ask Shelter, Citizens Advice or the council.

::: {.warn}
If a landlord asks for sex, sexual favours or says that "special arrangements" are needed to keep your home, this is unlawful and serious. Leave if you can do so safely, keep the messages, and report it to the police on 101, or 999 if you are in danger. Support is available from specialist services and from Shelter and Citizens Advice.
:::

::: {.example title="Saying no to an unannounced entry"}
A tenant returns from work to find her landlord inside, saying he "wanted to check the boiler". She stays calm and says: "Please leave and arrange a visit in writing with 24 hours' notice." The next day she emails the agent to confirm the rule, and keeps a record. The landlord does not return unannounced, and the agent apologises.
:::

### Mistakes that cost people money or time

- Leaving because a landlord says you must, without checking the process.
- Withholding rent as a protest. This can give the landlord a ground to evict.
- Not keeping a record of events.
- Waiting too long to report a problem.
- Assuming that your immigration status removes your housing rights.
